# Vietnam Market Pack

**Read this file whenever the request or the output language is Vietnamese**, or the brief targets the Vietnamese market. The mechanism in SKILL.md is unchanged — Durex Vietnam ran the same playbook for over a decade, well enough that Vietnamese marketers nicknamed the brand **"thánh bắt trend"** ("the trend-jacking saint"). What changes is the material: which formulas exist in Vietnamese, the length budget, the calendar, the red lines, and the type.

> **On accuracy**: the corpus lines below are quoted from Vietnamese press and marketing-analysis coverage (source list at the end of README.vi.md's Vietnam section). Original fanpage posts may differ slightly in punctuation or line breaks. Study the **mechanism**; don't recycle the lines.

---

## 1. Why double-layered meaning is load-bearing in Vietnam

In China the double layer is a good tactic. In Vietnam it is close to **the only move available**:

- **Traditional channels are effectively closed.** Condom advertising rarely reaches TV, print, or mass OOH; virtually all communication weight sits on social, where the brand owns its own content.
- **"Thuần phong mỹ tục" (public decency) is a real legal clause**, not a taste preference. Explicit content isn't just criticized — it gets taken down and fined. So **the inner layer must live in the reader's head, never on the page.** The iron law "explain it and it dies" is, in Vietnam, also a compliance requirement.
- **The barrier is embarrassment, not price.** Category penetration has been reported around 19%. Laughter is the cheapest tool for removing the embarrassment; that is the strategic job of the surface layer.

**Speed discipline**: England–Panama took 1 hour; Croatia's final took 24. Trend windows in Vietnam are measured in hours; a great line posted two days late is worth zero.

## 3. Formula mapping for Vietnamese

| # | Formula | Status in Vietnamese |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Homophone / polysemy | ★ **Stronger than in Chinese.** Monosyllabic language, dense polysemy: *bao, hàng, vào, sâu, đỉnh, chất, căng, nước, mềm, cứng* |
| 2 | Number pun | ★ Works as-is ("2 vạch", "1 hiệp", "triệu bàn thua") |
| 3 | Sense hijacking | ★★ **Biggest advantage.** The Vietnamese idiom/proverb/ca dao stock is enormous and universally memorized — bend one word and the whole known sentence snaps into the new meaning |
| 4 | Scene transplant | ★ Works as-is ("Nắp đậy vừa vặn, mực căng không tràn") |
| 5 | Character decomposition | ✗ **Does not exist** — quốc ngữ has no radicals. Use the three native replacements below |
| 6 | Parallelism / almanac do-and-don't | ★★ **Native format.** Tết couplets (câu đối), lịch vạn niên, "nên làm – kiêng làm" are things Vietnamese readers grew up reading |
| 7 | Verse | ★★ **Lục bát, vè, rap.** Vietnamese rap is a continuously flowing trend source, and everyone can scan lục bát meter |
| 8 | Inverted restraint | ★ Works as-is, and **matters more** — the Vietnamese calendar has more solemn dates (see §6) |

### Three native replacements for Formula 5

**a. Nói lái (spoonerism).** Vietnamese's signature word game — swapping syllable parts/tones so a phrase carries a hidden second reading (classic clean example: *đấu tranh → tránh đâu*). As strong as character decomposition is in Chinese, **and also the single biggest trap**: nói lái collapses into open obscenity in one step, which violates "suggest, never show" and invites takedowns. **Usage law: let the reader do the flipping — never flip it for them.** If the obscene reading is already spelled out on the page, the line is dead.

**b. Tone-mark play.** Adding, dropping, or switching diacritics to create the second reading (bàn / bán / bạn). Writing without diacritics is native chat culture, so the device reads natural rather than contrived.

**c. Hán–Việt vs. thuần Việt register.** Most concepts exist twice — a formal Sino-Vietnamese register and an everyday native one. Put the formal register on the page ("độc chiếm", "khai bút") and the reader translates downward to the everyday meaning themselves. This is the Vietnamese build of the **restrained voice**: the more solemn the diction, the harder the jump lands.

## 4. Length budget

The ≤12-Chinese-character rule converts to **6–10 Vietnamese words (~25–40 characters incl. spaces)** for a poster headline. Past that, the type shrinks and the 1.65–1.75× keyword scaling stops working. Every corpus line above fits the budget.

## 5. Voice and pronouns (diction supplement)

- **Default to subjectless headlines.** Vietnamese drops subjects naturally ("Cản phá như Lâm", "Lần đầu vào sâu") — this keeps both layers open and matches the corpus.
- **Pronoun choice is itself a register lever**: *bạn* = neutral-you; *em/anh* instantly sets an intimate frame (which can BE the inner layer, as in "về cho em hưởng"); *mình* = cozy first-person plural.
- **Never mày/tao** in brand voice — reads as vulgar, not edgy.

## 7. Vietnam red lines (added on top of SKILL.md's five)

1. **"Thuần phong mỹ tục" is legal risk, not taste.** Explicit content gets removed and fined — the technical reason the inner layer must stay off the page.
2. **Never touch politics, leadership, religion, or sovereignty.** There is no "clever" version of this.
3. **No jokes during national mourning, disasters, or floods.** Formula 8 or silence.
4. **Obscene nói lái is a hard red line**, even when the page text is technically "clean."
5. **Real people — including footballers the whole country is celebrating — only at the level of praising performance.** "Cản phá như Lâm" praises an actual save; it does not sexualize Đặng Văn Lâm personally. The gap between those two is narrow, and crossing it ends the brand.
6. **Football: praise, never mock.** Celebrate the win, console the loss. Mocking a foreign opponent is the shortest path to an online diplomatic incident.

## 8. Type and typesetting for Vietnamese

- **Diacritics are the failure mode.** Vietnamese stacks marks (ế = ê + sắc; ậ, ằ, ữ…), and uppercase **keeps** its diacritics. Before committing to any face — especially one you already use for Chinese or Latin work — render the test string **「Ắ ậ ằ ễ ỡ ộ ừ Đ đ」**: many display faces lack the precomposed glyphs entirely or fake them with misaligned combining marks (which breaks in Canvas rendering).
- **Safe free defaults** (SIL OFL / free for commercial use, full Vietnamese coverage): **Be Vietnam Pro** (designed for Vietnamese; good headline weights), the **Noto Sans / Noto Serif** families, **Inter**, **Montserrat**, **Lora**. On Google Fonts, filter by the **Vietnamese subset** before picking anything else.
- **Give lines air.** Stacked tone marks need more vertical clearance than Chinese or plain Latin — increase line-height and check that ascenders' marks don't clip against the line above, particularly at poster headline sizes.
- The SKILL.md newsjacking rule is unchanged: **the typeface follows the thing you're riding** — but verify the mimicked style's face still passes the diacritics test, or pair it with a Vietnamese-capable face for the copy layer.

## 9. Delivery test, restated for Vietnamese

Unchanged from SKILL.md, worth restating because nói lái tempts violations: **read the line literally. If it is still a clean, complete, factually sensible sentence about the surface event, it passes. If you have to explain it — or if the dirty reading is the literal reading — it fails.**
